Treating a prop-firm challenge as an option

What this branch is for, how the simulator works, and what we learned about the contract itself. The trading signals that feed it are deliberately left out.

1. The idea

A prop firm such as FTMO sells an evaluation. You pay a one-off fee, trade a simulated account, and if you hit the profit target without breaking the loss limits you get a funded account that pays out most of its profits (90 % at FTMO).

The payoff is asymmetric. If the evaluation fails, the loss stops at the fee. If it passes, the funded account can keep paying, with no cap, for months. That looks like a call option. This branch asks one question: what is a challenge worth, in dollars, per attempt and per month?

EV per attempt = P(pass) × V(funded account) − fee

The research on this branch evaluates a contract, not a market edge. That is why it sits outside the project's global trial counter. The first pre-registration (n° 18) used trades whose direction was a coin flip, to see how much of the value comes from the contract alone.

3. The FTMO 1-Step rules we model

The rules were read on ftmo.com and checked again during the work. When the website and the simulator disagreed, the simulator was fixed.

RuleWhat it means
Profit target+10 % of the starting balance to pass the evaluation. No minimum number of days, no time limit.
Daily lossEquity may not fall more than 3 % of the initial balance below the previous midnight balance.
Max loss10 %, trailing the highest end-of-day balance. On the 1-Step funded account it fully resets after each reward. This is the clause that makes the option valuable.
Best-day ruleThe best day may not exceed 50 % of the profit. Accounts ordered since 12 Oct 2026 measure this against net profit; older ones measure it against the sum of winning days only.

News rule (funded)No order may execute, stops and targets included, within 2 minutes of major US releases (NFP, CPI, advance GDP, Fed). Breaking it closes the account.
Fees10k account €79, 100k account €499. The fee is not refunded.

4. How the simulator works

Rules engine

Plain Python and numpy (propfirm/ftmo.py, propfirm/generic.py). Thousands of accounts run in parallel, all figures in % of the initial balance. Every rule above is applied at the end of each trade and each day.

Market paths

Real 1-minute NQ futures bars. Each simulated account starts on a random historical day and then walks forward in order, so volatility clusters and losing streaks look like the real ones.

Execution

One bracket trade per session. The stop fills at the worse of the stop price and the bar open. If the stop and target are hit in the same minute, it counts as a loss. CFD spread is charged on every trade.

Cross-check

The market side was rebuilt with vectorbt (Portfolio.from_signals) and gave the same numbers. A binary control mode (win +k or lose −1) reproduces the closed-form case.

Three trade sources

  • baseline Random direction: coin-flip long or short. It measures what the contract is worth with no skill at all.
  • research Historical trade lists: the realised R of each trade from the project's own rules, replayed through the contract. These rules are not described here.
  • replay Day-by-day replay of real recent sessions, to check one specific start date against the exact current rules.

What gets tuned

Only money management inside the contract: risk per trade on the evaluation, risk on the funded account, the reward-to-risk ratio, and how the target shrinks to the profit still needed. Settings are chosen on one period (for example 2011-2018) and reported on a later one (2019-2026).

What gets reported

  • Pass rate and sessions needed per evaluation.
  • Value of a funded account and probability of at least one reward.
  • EV per attempt (10k and 100k), and EV per month of account slot, which matters more when you can only run a few accounts.
  • Worst streaks: failed evaluations in a row, longest stretch with no reward.
  • The reward-refusal rate at which the EV drops to zero.

5. What we learned about the contract

Even with zero skill, the attempt is worth something. With coin-flip trades the first study found about 41 % of evaluations passing and an EV of roughly +$160 per 10k attempt and +$1,900 per 100k attempt. The value comes from the capped downside and the max-loss reset.

  • Lower risk wins. Across ten firms, the lowest risk setting gave the highest EV almost everywhere. Aggressive sizing makes evaluations much shorter but costs 30 to 40 % of the EV. Speed comes from risk, not from skill.
  • The contract matters more than the market. CFD firms with a max-loss reset after payout (FTMO, FundingPips) beat futures firms by a wide margin under the same trade stream.
  • The funded plan matters more than the evaluation plan. How you trade once funded drives most of the value.
  • Payout policy is a real lever. On the funded account, aim only for the profit the best-day rule still needs, then wait for the reward date instead of pushing further. Waiting does most of the work.
  • Several accounts on the same signal do not diversify. They blow up together and re-synchronise. Accounts on different signals spread out the reward dates.
  • The spread is a key assumption. With tight intraday stops, a CFD spread of 1 basis point can halve the EV.
  • Scaling up. Moving to the next account size only once rewards cover the worst historical drawdown of that size kept extra out-of-pocket money near zero. FTMO caps allocation per trader at 400k.

7. Metrics with the mom edge, under the target + wait policy

mom is one of the project's overnight NQ rules. Its definition is not given here. It trades on most nights, so a single account slot stays busy. The figures use the latest funded-account policy, target + wait, under FTMO's current best-day rule (accounts ordered since 12 Oct 2026: best day ≤ 50 % of net profit):

  • Target: if the account is in profit and only the best-day rule blocks a reward, the next trade aims only at the missing amount (2 × best day − profit, + 0.01 %) instead of the usual 2 R. It counts as hit if the trade's maximum favourable excursion reaches it.
  • Wait: as soon as the account is in profit and the rule holds, stop trading until the reward date (the 14th day after the cycle's first trade), then withdraw. A fresh account starts the next night.

The edge matters most on the funded side. The pass rate rises by about a third, but a funded account is worth 3.5 times more because it keeps collecting a small positive drift for months.

When the policy helps and when it does not

  • Modest or uncertain edge (2016-2026): +29 % EV per attempt and +8 % per month. Rewards are smaller (3.5 % against 4.3 %) but more frequent, and more accounts get paid.
  • Strong edge (2011-2015): neutral per attempt and −11 % per month. Waiting gives up nights with positive expectancy.
  • Under the older best-day rule (≤ 50 % of winning days only, accounts ordered before 12 Oct 2026) the rule is already loose, and shrinking the target costs money.

8. Caveats

Not modelled, or only partly.
  • Reward refusals or bans (for example FTMO's rule against "account rolling"). In the base case the EV only turns negative if roughly 65 % (10k) to 78 % (100k) of rewards are refused.
  • The news rule was added late. Earlier figures that ignore it are about 10 % too optimistic.
  • The actual CFD spread and leverage at the firm are assumptions, not measurements.
  • Any figure that uses the project's own trade lists inherits the selection bias of those rules. Treat it as an upper bound. The mom rule was chosen on 2016-2026; 2011-2015 is its out-of-sample period.
  • In the policy simulation, the 14-day reward delay is approximated as 10 trading nights. The target is judged hit from 1-minute bars, and a new account is assumed to start the night after a reward.
  • Worst historical streaks (6 failed evaluations in a row, about 11 months without a reward in 2022) were measured with the earlier funded plan, not with target + wait.
  • A live replay of September 2026 produced no reward under the current rules by 3 November. Historical averages hide long dry spells; the worst historical gap was about 11 months without a reward.
